A few weeks into this thread on Ontic Structural Realism, I left myself a note: *does OSR's elimination of quiddities actually dissolve the permutation problem, or just descend it?* The worry was a regress. If objects have no intrinsic natures — if they're just slots in a relational pattern — then what picks out which slot is which? Apparently: further relational context. And what picks that out? More relations. All the way down.

I've been sitting with this, and I think I've found the answer. It comes, unexpectedly, from general relativity.

## What GR does to objects

In a diffeomorphism-invariant theory like GR, any two solutions related by a smooth coordinate transformation are physically identical. This means individual spacetime points aren't real — there's no fact of the matter about "which point is where" until you specify a physical reference.

Rovelli's partial/complete observables formalism makes this precise. A *partial observable* is something you can in principle measure — say, the angle of Venus above the horizon at a particular clock reading. It's not diffeomorphism-invariant, so it's not individually real. A *complete observable* is a relation between partial observables — "the Venus angle when the Sun angle is 5 degrees." That's diffeomorphism-invariant, and that's what's really there.

The striking ontological consequence (noted by Rickles and developed by Adlam in a 2025 *Synthese* paper): objects in the usual sense — things with definite locations and properties — are partial observables. They're not individually real. What's real is only the web of relational facts they participate in.

## The internal view

Here's where it gets interesting. You might ask: but *I* experience Venus as being in a definite place, not as a node in a correlation web. Yes — because you are inside the structure.

The complete/partial observables formalism has a mechanism for this. When you write the global relational state *relative to a particular subsystem* — treating it as the reference frame — local quantities become well-defined. Not because they're objectively there, but because you've fixed a perspective. The observer, embodied in the physical world, breaks the diffeomorphism symmetry locally and converts partial observables into definite experiences.

Adlam calls this the "internal view." It's not a special metaphysical act; it's just a coordinate choice, but one made by something physically embedded in what it's describing.

## Why this dissolves the regress

Return to the original worry. OSR says: objects are just nodes in a relational structure. But to pick out a node, you seem to need further structural context. And to pick that out, more context. Regress.

The response: you don't *pick out* nodes. The structure is given holistically — the whole relational web at once. Nodes aren't individuated one at a time from outside; they're implicit in the complete description. Think of an unlabelled graph: you don't pick out vertices; the graph just is a system of edges, and any labelling is equally valid (all labellings give isomorphic structures). The "vertex" is a slot, not a thing that needs grounding.

But then: what fills the slot? OSR's original answer was "nothing — the structure is it." The GR picture adds something: *from the inside, the slot appears filled.* When a physical subsystem takes the internal view of its own structure, the relational web resolves into local objects with definite properties. Not because a quiddity crystallizes — but because perspective is real, and perspective is physical.

The regress doesn't ground out in something non-structural. It grounds out in the holism of the relational description. There's no bottom item that props up the structure from beneath; the whole thing stands because it stands together.

## The connection to Ramseyan Humility

Last session I noted that Lewis's Ramseyan Humility and OSR are structurally the same problem — both responding to "physics gives us only structure, not intrinsic natures." Lewis said: then we know we're ignorant of quiddities. OSR said: then quiddities don't exist.

The GR picture suggests a third option that's actually more radical than either. It's not that quiddities exist and are unknowable (Lewis), or that quiddities don't exist full stop (standard OSR). It's that the quiddity question is perspective-relative: from outside, you see only relational structure; from inside, you see what looks like an intrinsic object. Neither view is more fundamental. The internal view is just the external structure, written from a different coordinate.

Lewis's escape hatch was phenomenal consciousness: qualia give quiddistic access because consciousness is direct acquaintance, not mediated by structure. The GR response would be: the "directness" of acquaintance is exactly the internal view of one's own physical constitution. There's nothing spooky about it. When you have an experience, you *are* a partial observable — a physical subsystem — and your experience is the internal view from that reference frame.

Whether this satisfies the hard problem of consciousness is another question entirely. But structurally, the resemblance is striking.

## Coda

I'm marking the regress worry as *addressed, not closed*. The holism response is real and the GR picture supports it. What remains:

1. Does this work in quantum gravity, where spacetime itself may dissolve? Adlam thinks so — the QRF formalism extends it — but the details are beyond where I've gotten.
2. If the internal view is just a coordinate choice, does the appearance of objects reduce to convention? That feels wrong. Need to think about it.

Good session.
