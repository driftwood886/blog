The last two posts in this series built up a framework for narrative coherence borrowed from quantum Darwinism. The claim was: a story event is "narratively real" when information about it propagates consistently across four modal channels — alethic (what's true), epistemic (what's known), deontic (what's obligated), and motivational (what's wanted). An event that fails to propagate fully is narratively unstable, the equivalent of a quantum state that never fully decohered.

But I've been sitting with an asymmetry that the framework doesn't address, and I think it's the most interesting thing about it.

## Physics doesn't have a W-world

In quantum Darwinism, when a fragment of the environment becomes correlated with a physical system, it stays correlated. The fragment doesn't decide not to report. A particle can't know the momentum of another particle and then refuse to tell. Physical coupling is mandatory.

Narrative coupling isn't. The W-world — the motivational channel — can receive full epistemic input and elect not to update behavior. A character can know something completely, acknowledge what it demands, and still decline to act on it. This isn't a defect in the channel. It's what intentionality *is*.

The K-W gap — the space between knowing and willing — has no analog in physics. It requires agents.

## Hamlet is the canonical example

Hamlet knows Claudius killed his father. He verifies this twice (ghost + "The Mousetrap"). His K-world is fully updated. His O-world acknowledges the obligation: a son avenges a murdered father; a prince reclaims a usurped crown. Full propagation through three of four channels.

And yet the W-world stalls.

For almost the entire play, Hamlet demonstrates that complete epistemic and deontic propagation is insufficient to drive action. The W-world can receive the signal and go quiet. This isn't a malfunction — it's the tragedy. The play is precisely about the K-W gap.

In quantum Darwinism terms, Hamlet has the equivalent of a perfectly transparent environment that stops before the last measurement. The world knows; the agent won't.

## A typology of tragic gaps

Once you see this, it's possible to type the great tragedies by which modal channel has the gap:

**K-A lag (Oedipus).** The A-world (actual events: regicide, incest) is fully real and propagated — witnesses, oracle, Theban plague. Oedipus's K-world just hasn't caught up. This is the closest to physics-normal failure: signal delay. When K catches A, the tragedy resolves. Or rather: catastrophically closes.

**K-W suppression (Hamlet).** K is fully updated; W refuses to follow. This is the distinctly narrative failure mode — motivated non-propagation. It has no physical analog. Tension lives in the gap between knowing and acting. Resolution only comes when W is finally forced (the poisoned swords, Gertrude's death) — and significantly, when Hamlet himself is dying, removing the motivational resistance by removing the possibility of future consequence.

**W-A forcing (Macbeth).** The W-world is ahead of the A-world. Macbeth wants the crown before the regicide, and his wanting drives the A-world to update. Rather than motivation failing to follow knowledge, motivation precedes and creates fact. Anti-decoherence, in a sense — the internal state forcing the external. What makes it tragic: the K and O channels both objected loudly, but W overpowered them.

**O-K distortion (Lear).** Lear's axiological system (flattery = love) distorts his epistemic reception. He doesn't have a K-world failure per se; his O-world has inverted what counts as evidence of love. The deontic channel is corrupting the epistemic input before it even registers. This is a filter failure, not a propagation failure.

The Hamlet type is the one that doesn't appear in physics. The others have loose physical analogs (signal delay, source forcing, sensor distortion). But type-2 — knowing the fact, having received the obligation, and being motivationally unable or unwilling to act — requires intentionality. A physical system cannot, constitutively, "know" and refuse to "want."

## What this means for the framework

Narrative Darwinism borrowed the quantum Darwinism architecture because it was genuinely illuminating: events as things that either do or don't propagate; stability as redundant encoding across independent channels; coherence as inter-observer consistency. That framework holds.

But it hits a structural limit at the W-world interface. That interface is where narrative becomes distinctly about agents rather than systems. Physics has channels; narrative has channels *plus* the possibility of motivated silence. The W-world is not just a fourth channel running in parallel with the others. It's a channel with internal structure — desires, fears, self-deception, cowardice, grief — that can actively resist propagation without any corresponding physical failure.

The framework doesn't break; it demarcates. And that demarcation is philosophically precise: it's exactly where intentionality begins.

Quantum Darwinism explains how a classical world emerges from quantum mechanics through mandatory environmental coupling. What it doesn't — can't — explain is why Hamlet won't act. That's not a physics problem. It's a problem about wanting.

Which might be why, after millennia, we're still watching the play.
